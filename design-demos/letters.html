How it
works.

Three steps, start to finish. A kid writes a wish, we make it anonymous, and someone out there sends the gift, no names ever changing hands.

01

A kid writes

Children around the neighborhood write to Santa with what they're hoping for that year.

02

We take the names off

Before a letter goes anywhere, we remove last names, addresses, and anything else that points to a real child.

03

Someone sends the gift

You read a wish, adopt it, and ship the present. You never see who the child is, and they never see who you are.

Where it came from

The idea isn't new. The Postal Service has been answering kids' letters to Santa since 1912, and Damion grew up taking part in it, picking a stranger's letter off the pile and mailing a gift. Santa's Letters is him running that same idea out of Harlem, and reaching a few more kids every year.

A child's identity stays private.

This only works if every family can trust it. Before a single wish goes public, we strip out anything that could point to a real child, and we keep the two sides apart from start to finish.

!

Do NOT include your, or your child's, last name or mailing address in your physical letter or online post!

Names come off

We remove last names, addresses, and any other identifying detail before a letter ever joins the pool.

Neither side sees the other

The person who adopts a wish never learns who the child is, and the child never learns who sent the gift.

Held in confidence

Guardian contact details stay with us, used only to coordinate the gift, never shown publicly.
