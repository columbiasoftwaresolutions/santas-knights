Santa's Knights' mission is to bring free martial arts, fitness, and activities to everyone, equitably, transcending socioeconomic, racial, and location boundaries, positively changing children's and adults' lives through exposure and lifestyle enhancement.

How a kid who once adopted a stranger's letter built a nonprofit that does two things at once.

01

A letter, picked
off a pile

As a kid, Damion DiGrazia took part in the Postal Service's Operation Santa: you adopt a stranger's letter to Santa and send the gift they asked for. It stuck with him. Years later, Santa's Letters became his way of doing the same thing for Harlem, on a bigger scale.

02

The training
came first

In 2013 he started Gladiators NYC, full-contact armored combat with steel weapons. It grew into the oldest league of its kind in the city. In 2016 he made it a nonprofit so the classes could be free, and the giving could have a home.

03

One organization,
two jobs

Today Santa's Knights does two things out of Harlem. It runs free martial arts and fitness all year, and every holiday season it answers kids' letters to Santa. The training has its own site now. This one is for everything else.

Damion DiGrazia
Founder, Santa's Knights

Damion DiGrazia is a service-disabled military veteran and fourth-generation New Yorker. He came home from the service badly injured — martial arts and fitness were what pulled him back. He had been teaching martial arts for over five years when, inspired by receiving a thoughtful gift through Operation Santa Claus as a child, he set out to build a free platform for people to pursue their best selves through martial arts and fitness.

He brought a career on Wall Street and degrees from Ivy League universities to what became a full-time commitment to community. He left finance to build Santa's Knights — giving people the two things that had helped him most: a reason to move, and people to do it with.

He started Gladiators NYC in 2013, turning it into the Santa's Knights 501(c)(3) nonprofit. The Letters to Santa program came from his own childhood, adopting a stranger's letter through Operation Santa, and he has been widening it every December since.

“I'm ex-military and I got heavily injured while I was in. When I came out I was just a mess. Fitness and sports really saved my life.”
Damion DiGraziaTo CBS New York

What we stand on.

Four things that don't change, whatever the season.

01Nobody pays

Classes, the holiday gifts, the events. The people who use them never see a bill. Donations and grants cover the rest.

02Open to whoever shows up

Kids, adults, women, veterans. You don't need experience, money, or a reason to be here.

03We work in Harlem

Our home is the Manhattanville Community Center on 133rd Street, and most of what we do happens within walking distance of it.

04Giving is the point

The training is what we're known for. Santa's Letters and the community work are why the nonprofit exists.
